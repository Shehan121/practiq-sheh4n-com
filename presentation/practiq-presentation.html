01 — Naming

Why "Practiq"?

Cut "-ikum," paste "-iq" — the name is Praktikum + IQ: applying to internships in a smarter, more meaningful way, not just a faster one.

Praktikumiq
  • 01
    Praktikum

    German for "mandatory internship" — the exact requirement almost every non-EU CS student has to clear to stay on track for graduation.

  • 02
    + IQ

    The swapped ending borrows "IQ" — intelligence. Not "apply to everything," but apply with judgment: only to what actually fits your visa, your German level, and your stack.

  • 03
    Why it fits

    Praktikum names the goal; IQ names the method. Together they describe exactly what the app does — a wiser way to search, not just another job board.

02 — Brand Validation

A clever name is only good if it's actually free.

Before treating "Practiq" as final, it was checked against the three places a name can quietly collide with someone else's: the German trademark register, domain registries, and social handles. Two real conflicts turned up.

DPMA · Trademark
Search pending

register.dpma.de

The DPMA's word-mark register was located and is searchable by class — a formal query against Nice Class 9 (software) and 42 (SaaS) hasn't been filed yet. No result to report either way; this is the next concrete step before any commercial filing.

Domains
Conflict found

practiq.ch — taken

practiq.ch already belongs to an unrelated Swiss healthcare company (a CIRS incident-reporting network for medical practices, founded 2024). Different country and industry, but the exact name. practiq.de / .app / .io still need a live registrar check.

Social Handles
Handles taken

@practiq — taken (x2)

Instagram's @practiq belongs to an unrelated personal account. LinkedIn's /company/practiq is the same Swiss healthcare business as above. Fallback handles like @practiq.app or @usepractiq are the safer bet.

Prior art found: "PractiQ" already operates in Swiss healthcare (practiq.ch) — different market, same name, worth a legal look before launch

04 — Design Principle

Filter once, at onboarding — not on every screen after.

  • 01
    Collect context upfront

    Name, visa status and language level are captured in "Begin your journey" before Amir sees a single listing.

  • 02
    System does the elimination

    Incompatible listings are removed structurally — Trade Republic's Android role, for example, is flagged visa-incompatible automatically, never left for Amir to discover mid-application.

  • 03
    Every later screen inherits trust

    Home, Matches, Job Detail and the Applications tracker never re-ask what onboarding already knows.

05 — The Payoff

Rank the matches. Don't list them.

  • 01
    Compatibility score, not a wall of text

    A single percentage — 96%, 91%, 88% — replaces the need to read every listing to judge fit.

  • 02
    Fewer, better choices

    Hick's Law: reducing decision complexity speeds up the choice itself. "Rare" badges flag the standout matches.

  • 03
    Scale, stated plainly

    4k+ students matched, 120 partner companies, a 73% interview rate — the numbers Home leads with.

08 — Emotional Design

Turning application anxiety into a game worth returning to.

  • 01
    XP and Level replace a blank checklist

    Every action earns something — +50 XP on apply, +30 XP even on a rejection. Level 4, a 7-day streak and a 98% "Profile Strength" bar turn admin into visible progress.

  • 02
    Quest framing, everywhere

    "Today's quest," "Your quests," "Begin your journey" — the vocabulary reframes a stressful, high-stakes task as a game Amir is actively progressing through, not a chore list judging him.

  • 03
    Peak-End Rule, deployed at the highest-anxiety moment

    Kahneman's Peak-End Rule: people judge an experience by its most intense point and how it ends — not the average. Hitting "submit" on an application is Amir's single most anxious moment in the app. Practiq answers it immediately with the Celebration screen — confetti, a checkmark badge, "+30 XP" — making that peak a designed high, not a silent form post.

  • 04
    Even the worst outcome gets a soft landing

    A rejection still reads "+30 XP... Keep Applying!!" — the one moment most likely to end a session badly is deliberately re-scored as forward progress, protecting the end-of-session feeling Amir walks away with.

Maps to: Peak-End Rule (Kahneman), Self-Determination Theory

09 — Physical Prototype

A deadline you can see without opening the app.

Practiq extends past the screen into a small desk object — an ergonomic, decorative "deadline companion" that sits on Amir's desk like any other desk item, not another notification demanding attention.

  • 01
    One glance, one match

    A small built-in screen shows only the rarest, highest-match internship currently open, plus the days left to apply — no scrolling, no app to unlock.

  • 02
    Three lights, one month

    A traffic-light column tracks urgency across roughly a month: green while there's comfortable runway, yellow as it narrows, red as the deadline closes in.

  • 03
    Decorative, not demanding

    It's designed to be kept freely on a desk as an ambient object — deadline awareness happens passively, in the room, instead of through a phone that has to be picked up and unlocked.

12 — Colour System

Every colour is doing a job, not decorating one.

The obvious choice was blue — it's what LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor and every other job board already use. That's exactly why it was rejected: blue is the colour of the bureaucracy Amir is trying to escape, not the feeling Practiq is trying to create.

Why not blue
  • 01
    Lime is the only action colour

    Reserved for primary CTAs, XP rewards and the brand mark itself. Nothing else on screen uses it, so "what do I tap next" is never ambiguous.

  • 02
    Purple means "trust this number"

    Match percentages and emphasis words like "GET HIRED" use purple specifically so a statistic is never mistaken for a button — lime says "do," purple says "here's a fact."

  • 03
    Black, thick borders — legibility over subtlety

    Every card is outlined, not just shadowed. Contrast holds up whether Amir is scanning between classes or reading in a dim room.

  • 04
    Identical in light and dark mode

    Lime, purple and salmon are defined once and never overridden per theme — colour keeps the same meaning regardless of which mode Amir is in.

  • 05
    Purple over blue, deliberately

    Blue already means two conflicting things in most people's heads — "link" and "info." Purple carries none of that baggage, so a match score never gets mistaken for something tappable.
